============
Known Issues
============

- With ``--specular include_specular``, BornAgain puts the specular reflection into the bin of the outgoing-direction grid that contains the specular direction, and the ray of that bin goes to the (randomly shifted) bin centre. The simulated specular spot is therefore smeared uniformly over one grid bin: with a coarse grid it is much wider than the measured one (for the D22 measurement of silica nanoparticles on silicon included in the repository (``data/paper``, the data of the mcstas_gisans paper, see :doc:`cite`): an RMS width of 1.1 pixels with bins of 3.5 pixels, instead of 0.57 pixel; measured: 0.62), and with a very low number of directions this appears as a large artefact. BornAgain also *replaces* the diffuse intensity of that bin by the reflectivity instead of adding it, so the diffuse scattering of one bin per neutron is lost; near the Yoneda angle and with a coarse grid this is noticeable (the same data, 37 × 19 directions over the whole detector: 11% of the specular intensity). ``--specular specular_simulation`` sends the specular reflection as one extra ray per neutron hitting the sample, in the exact mirror direction, with the reflectivity of a separate BornAgain specular simulation that uses the same options (``--use_avg_materials``, polarisation and analyser), so its position and width do not depend on the grid (0.56 pixel with both grids, with the same intensity) and no diffuse intensity is lost. Cross-checked on several D22 measurements (the data in ``data/paper`` and measurements of bare D2O, nanoparticles and microgels at the air/D2O interface) against the expected specular counts (sum of weight × reflectivity of the neutrons hitting the sample): the reflected intensity agrees exactly, and the specular widths agree with the measured ones. A fit region that excludes the specular spot with a margin of more than half a grid bin is not affected by the choice; ``specular_simulation`` is recommended. (The transmitted part of the beam, 1 − reflectivity, enters the substrate and is not simulated.)

- The detection process is not simulated: the detection efficiency is 100%, without scattering in the detector, dead time or attenuators. Only the position resolution (``resolution`` in the instrument definition) and the pixelation are applied.

- The detector is assumed to be flat and perpendicular to the beam axis (see :doc:`technical_details`).

- Simulation with polarisation (``--use_polarization`` and the analyser options of ``mg_run``) is implemented, but not validated against measurements yet. The tests cover only the consistency of the two specular options with a polarised beam and an analyser (the analyser is set with the BornAgain 22+ interface, with a fallback for older versions).

- Fitting of TOF data (``mg_fit`` with a TOF instrument) is not implemented yet.
